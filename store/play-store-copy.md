# Google Play listing copy

This is the canonical source for the Google Play Store listing text. It
didn't live in any tracked file before, the original version only existed as
pasted text in a chat brief, so it's captured here now to stay in sync with
the website copy going forward. This is Android-specific by nature (a Play

## Store fields

**App title** (30/30 characters, no room left)

symetric: Track Between Visits

**Short description** (74/80 characters)

Two minutes a day. In six weeks, get the whole picture for your clinician.

**Category and rating**

Health & Fitness &middot; Rated for 18+ &middot; One-off payment, no ads, no in-app purchases

## Full description

Your appointment is fifteen minutes long and covers the last three months or more.

Nobody can do that from memory. You answer based on the last few days, because that's what's top of mind, and your ups and downs back in February and your lack of motivation most mornings don't make it into the room. Then your next three months are evaluated on incomplete information.

It's not a memory problem. It's a data problem, and it happens to all of us.

symetric is a small, quiet fix for it. I built it because I kept walking out of appointments having forgotten to say what I wanted to say, and I use it every day.

**WHAT IT ASKS OF YOU**

A few times a day, during hours you choose, it asks how you're doing in the areas you picked. Thirty seconds at a time. Tap, tap, done.

You choose the domains you want to track: mood, energy, anxiety, concentration, irritability, social depletion, sensory overwhelm, motivation. Track two of them or all eight. You can change your mind whenever you like.

You choose the hours, and how many times a day. Three is the default. Two is fine.

**WHAT HAPPENS WHEN YOU MISS ONE**

Nothing. There's no streak to break, no flashing lights, no angry red pop-up.

If you're having a rough fortnight, the app won't punish you for it, and the fortnight without data itself is data worth having. Pick it back up when you can.

**WHAT YOU GET, AND ROUGHLY WHEN**

Week 1: you're just logging. Nothing to read yet.

Week 3: it knows your baseline and shows you what normal looks like for you, not for anyone else.

Week 6: it starts recognising patterns. "Anxiety is worse the day after poor sleep." "Wednesdays are consistently your lowest." "All areas dropped at once on 29 June. That's happened once."

By your next appointment: you have a record. Data to make the subjective a little more objective.

Export everything as CSV or JSON whenever you want. Delete any date range. Reset your baseline when your normal genuinely changes.

**FOR THE APPOINTMENT ITSELF**

Choose your date range. Add topics for discussion. Flag the patterns you want to show.

Then generate a PDF to hand over: summary, patterns, charts for each area, and what changed after each event you logged.

Afterwards, log how it went and what was discussed. If you want. Up to you.

**YOU DON'T NEED A DIAGNOSIS**

symetric is for people who know something isn't right but aren't sure how to put it into words. You might be on a waiting list, between clinicians, or have been told that "it's probably nothing". symetric doesn't ask what's wrong with you and doesn't need a label to work. It records what you report and shows you what it finds. Only data. Never assumptions.

**WHAT IT WON'T DO**

One-off payment, no subscription.

No identifying information needed beyond an email address.

No advice, no telling you to try journalling.

No AI analysing your every move, just maths running the numbers on the data you've provided.

symetric isn't a health device nor diagnostic tool and doesn't replace medical care. It just asks you to record your domains in real time.

## Screenshot captions

Most people never expand the description. These five captions are the real listing, written to be read as a sequence, so keep the order.

1. Fifteen minutes to describe the last three months? Nobody can do that.
2. So answer it thirty seconds at a time.
3. Miss one. Miss a week. Nothing breaks.
4. By about week six, it has something to say.
5. A PDF to hand across the desk.
